Hertfordshire

TMV servicing and testing in Hertfordshire

Hertfordshire's post-war new towns, commuter towns and chalk water give it a particular TMV profile: lots of older housing stock with adapted bathrooms, plenty of schools and care homes, and valves that scale up quickly.

A lot of Hertfordshire was planned. Letchworth and Welwyn Garden City were early garden cities, and Stevenage, Hemel Hempstead and Hatfield were built or expanded as new towns after the Second World War. A lot of their housing went up in the 1950s and 60s, and plenty of it is still owned by councils and housing associations. That shapes the TMV work here more than anything else.

New-town housing and adapted bathrooms

Housing stock of that age has been through several rounds of improvement. Bathrooms have been adapted for older or disabled tenants, level-access showers fitted and TMVs added along the way, often by different contractors on different programmes. The result is a spread of valve makes and ages, some of them now obsolete, and records that are patchy at best.

When parts dry up for an old valve, replacement is usually the sensible call. Our article on Repair or replace? When an old TMV has had its day sets out how we decide. For a landlord with a few hundred homes, the first step is a survey that tags and logs each valve. After that, a rolling programme keeps everything tested without having to get into every home on the same day.

Sheltered schemes and care homes

Across the county there are sheltered housing schemes, extra care developments and care homes, from purpose-built blocks to extended period houses in the market towns. Communal assisted bathrooms, en-suites and residents' basins usually need TMV3 valves. HSE's HSIS6 sets a 44°C maximum for baths where vulnerable people are at risk, and many sites set showers and basins lower still.

Schools and nurseries

Hertfordshire has a lot of schools, with primaries, nurseries and special schools in and around the towns. Under-fives washrooms, changing areas and medical rooms are the usual places for TMVs. We fit testing around the school calendar, and it pairs well with the flushing that's needed when buildings reopen after a holiday. See TMVs in schools and nurseries: a practical guide for the practical side.

Healthcare and labs

Hospitals, community hospitals, hospices, GP surgeries and private clinics across the county follow HTM 04-01, which means TMV3 valves and documented in-service testing to the D 08 method. Hertfordshire also has science parks and research sites, where staff showers and welfare areas tend to be the forgotten valves. They don't need the same frequency as a ward, but they still need checking.

Chalk water

Affinity Water supplies most of Hertfordshire, drawing a lot of it from the chalk aquifer. The result is hard to very hard water across most of the county. Check your postcode on your supplier's site for the specifics.

Hard water is the single biggest reason Hertfordshire TMVs stop working well. Scale stiffens the thermostatic element and blocks strainers, and the valve gets slower to shut down when the cold is isolated. We clean strainers and descale at every service, and record which valves are worst so the interval can be shortened where needed.

Getting to you

From Chiswick we pick up the North Circular and then the M1, A1(M) or M25 depending on where you are. The A10 covers the east of the county and the A41 the west. Most sites have parking, though town centre buildings in St Albans or Hertford can be trickier, so tell us about access when you book.

Hertfordshire FAQs

Questions from Hertfordshire sites

Do you cover the whole of Hertfordshire?

Yes, we cover the whole county, from Watford and Borehamwood in the south to Royston and Hitchin in the north. We travel out from Chiswick, and for landlords or trusts with lots of buildings we plan visits town by town so each trip covers as much as possible.

Is Hertfordshire water hard?

Yes, most of Hertfordshire has hard to very hard water. Affinity Water supplies most of the county, much of it from chalk. For TMVs that means faster scaling of the thermostatic element and strainers, so descaling should be a routine part of each service rather than an occasional extra.

Our older TMVs can't get parts any more. What are the options?

Replacing the valve is usually the answer once parts are no longer made. We'll confirm whether any spares are still available first. If not, we'll quote for a modern TMV3 or TMV2 valve to suit the outlet, fit it close to the tap where we can, and commission it properly with records from day one.

Can you work through a whole housing stock gradually?

Yes. Many landlords prefer a rolling programme, where we survey and test a block or estate at a time, then keep each one on its own cycle. You get a single register for the whole stock, showing what's done, what's due and what's outstanding, without needing to access every home in one go.
