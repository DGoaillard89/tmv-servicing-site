Choosing a valve

TMV2 or TMV3?

They're both certification schemes rather than brands. The one you need depends on who's using the water and what kind of building it is.

Domestic-type use

TMV2

For homes and similar settings. Valves are tested against BS EN 1111 (high pressure) or BS EN 1287 (low pressure) and certified under the TMV2 scheme.

Typical use
Houses, flats, general housing, staff washrooms
Pressures
High or low pressure versions
Good for
Bath fills in dwellings, everyday outlets
Healthcare & care

TMV3

For healthcare and care settings, built to the NHS performance specification behind the TMV3 scheme, with tighter performance and fail-safe requirements.

Typical use
Hospitals, care homes, nursing homes, some schools and supported housing
Guidance
Expected for healthcare premises under HTM 04-01
Good for
Anywhere users may be vulnerable to scalding

Some valves are dual-certified for both TMV2 and TMV3. That can make life simpler on mixed sites. Read more in our TMV2 vs TMV3 guide.

Getting it right

What we think about before we fit anything

{{icon:care}}

Who uses the outlet

Residents, patients, young children and people with reduced feeling all point towards TMV3 and tighter set points.

{{icon:valve}}

The outlet type

A bath, shower, basin and bidet each have different typical maximum temperatures and flow needs.

{{icon:dial}}

Supply pressures

We check whether your system is high or low pressure, and whether pressures are balanced, so the valve can actually perform.

{{icon:bacteria}}

Distance to the outlet

Mixed water is in the Legionella growth range, so we fit valves as close to the outlet as practical to keep that pipe run short.

{{icon:wrench}}

Access for servicing

A valve sealed behind tiles is a valve nobody services. We fit with access panels in mind so testing stays easy.

{{icon:shield}}

Whether you need one at all

Your risk assessment should drive this. Sometimes a different control fits better, and we'll say so.

Retrofits

Adding TMVs to an existing building

Lots of buildings were fitted out before anyone thought about scalding risk, or have changed use since. A care home in a converted house, say, or a school that's added a nursery.

We retrofit TMVs under basins, behind bath panels and in shower risers, usually with very little disruption. Where there's space, a valve that's built into the tap or shower is often the neatest fix.

Commissioning

Set up properly, and written down

Commissioning gives every valve a baseline. Future tests are compared against it, so it's worth doing carefully.

  1. Supplies checked

    Hot and cold supply temperatures and pressures recorded at the valve.

  2. Set to the right maximum

    Mixed temperature set for the outlet type and the people using it.

  3. Fail-safe proved

    Cold supply isolated to confirm the valve shuts down as it should.

  4. Added to your register

    Tagged, logged and scheduled for its first in-service test.

Installation FAQs

Questions about fitting TMVs

Do I need a TMV on every outlet?

Not always. It depends on who uses the outlet and what your risk assessment says. In care and healthcare settings, outlets used by residents or patients will usually need one. In an office, a staff kitchen tap might not.

Can you fit a TMV under an existing basin?

Usually, yes. Under-basin TMVs are one of the most common retrofits. We just need enough space to fit it and get back to it for servicing.

Should I replace TMV2 valves with TMV3?

If the building now houses vulnerable people, or it's a healthcare setting, TMV3 is usually what's expected. Otherwise a working TMV2 valve in a domestic-type setting can be fine. We'll look at each outlet rather than recommending a blanket swap.

What happens after you install a valve?

We commission it, record the readings and add it to your asset register. Then it needs in-service checks in the first few months, and regular servicing after that.

When should a newly fitted TMV be tested again?

Annex F of the D 08 supplement suggests 6 to 8 weeks and again at 12 to 15 weeks after commissioning. New valves often settle in that time, and any debris left in fresh pipework tends to show up in the strainers. We'll book those dates in when we fit the valve, so they don't get missed.

How close to the outlet will you fit it?

As close as the space allows, and ideally in the tap or shower itself. HSG274 Part 2 asks for the shortest practical run of blended water, because warm water sitting in pipework is good for Legionella. We'll also make sure it can be reached for servicing without taking tiles off.
