Why TMVs matter

Two risks, pulling in opposite directions

Hot water has to be stored hot to keep Legionella in check. But water that hot can scald in seconds. A TMV lets you have both: hot in the pipes, safe at the tap.

10°C70°C

Cold stays cold

Cold water should be below 20°C. Between roughly 20°C and 45°C is where Legionella bacteria grow best.

Safe at the outlet

The TMV blends hot and cold to a set temperature, typically around 41°C for a shower and up to 44°C for a bath.

Hot stays hot

Stored hot water should be at least 60°C, which is well above the temperature that can cause a serious scald.

Compliance, simply

The rules, without the jargon

TMVs in healthcare are covered by HTM 04-01. Valves themselves are made to standards such as BS EN 1111 and BS EN 1287, and certified under the TMV2 or TMV3 schemes. Your Legionella risk assessment, under ACOP L8 and HSG274, should say where you need them.

We'll explain which bits apply to your building, and make sure your records show the work's been done.

Quick answers

Common questions

How often should a TMV be serviced?

At least once a year for most sites, and often every six months in care and healthcare buildings or hard water areas. Healthcare guidance (D 08, Annex F) has new valves retested at 6 to 8 weeks and 12 to 15 weeks, then spaces tests out based on how stable each valve proves to be. Our HTM 04-01 and D 08 guide has the detail.

What's the difference between TMV2 and TMV3?

Both are certification schemes. TMV2 covers valves for domestic-type use. TMV3 covers valves for healthcare and care settings, with tougher performance requirements. If you look after vulnerable people, you'll usually want TMV3.

Do you only work in London?

No. We cover the whole of the UK and specialise in London and the Home Counties.

Will I get a certificate?

Yes. Every valve we test gets its results recorded, and you get a certificate and an updated asset register after each visit.
