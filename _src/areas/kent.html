TMV servicing and testing in Kent

Kent sits on chalk, and its water shows it. Add a coastline lined with care homes and retirement housing, plus seaside hotels that close for part of the year, and you've got a county where TMVs need steady attention.

Kent is a big county with a lot of variety. North Kent and the Medway towns have dense Victorian and post-war housing. Mid Kent has market towns and business parks. The east Kent coast has resort towns popular with retired people, and the care sector reflects that. Different places, different valves, but one thing in common: hard water.

Chalk, hard water and scaled-up valves

Much of Kent's drinking water comes from the chalk, and it's hard to very hard across most of the county. Southern Water and South East Water supply most of it, with Affinity Water around Folkestone and Dover. Your supplier's website has a postcode checker if you want the detail for your building.

For thermostatic mixing valves, that hardness is the main enemy. Scale coats the thermostatic element so it reacts slowly, and flakes collect in the strainers until flow drops off. A valve that's been left for a couple of years on Kent water can be well past recalibrating and need a new cartridge. We'd usually suggest descaling at every service here, and our article on {{postlink:hard-water-and-tmvs}} explains what that involves.

Care homes along the coast

From Whitstable round to Folkestone and beyond, the coast has a lot of care homes, nursing homes and retirement housing. Residents with dementia or reduced sensation are exactly the people TMVs are there to protect. HSE's guidance on scalding and burning in health and social care is clear that where vulnerable people are at risk, hot water needs controlling at the outlet, and those controls need maintaining.

Plenty of these homes are converted seafront hotels or large Victorian villas. They often have a mix of valve types and ages, and valves tucked behind bath panels nobody has opened in years. A first visit usually means building a proper register before anything else.

Seasonal hotels and low use

Seaside hotels, guest houses and holiday accommodation often close or run at half capacity over winter. Water sitting in pipes for weeks is a Legionella concern under HSG274 Part 2, and it isn't kind to TMVs either. Before reopening, valves need flushing, testing and their strainers checked. {{postlink:tmvs-after-low-use}} goes through the steps.

Schools, academy trusts and housing

Kent's schools include grammar schools, non-selective secondaries, primaries and special schools, often grouped into multi-academy trusts that span several towns. Nursery washrooms, early years areas and SEN facilities are where TMVs usually turn up. We plan routes so one trip covers several schools, and keep the records under one register for the trust.

Councils and housing associations across Kent and Medway run sheltered schemes and supported housing. Getting into each flat needs coordinating with your housing team, and tenants appreciate notice. Clear records across the stock make it simple to show what's done and what's outstanding.

Healthcare

Hospitals, community health centres, hospices and private clinics across Kent work to HTM 04-01. That means TMV3 valves, documented in-service tests and a frequency your Water Safety Group can back up. We use the D 08 test method and record results per valve.

Getting to you

We drive out from Chiswick. The M25, M2, M20 and A2 get us across the county, and we keep the Dartford Crossing charge in mind for north Kent trips. Because Kent is large, we group visits by area (Medway and the north, mid Kent, or the east Kent coast) so your quote reflects sensible travel.

Kent FAQs

Questions from Kent sites

Do you cover east Kent and the coast?

Yes, we cover all of Kent, including Thanet, Dover, Folkestone and the rest of the coast. We travel out from Chiswick and group visits by area, so a trip to the coast usually takes in several sites. If you've got buildings in different parts of the county, we'll plan the order to keep travel sensible.

Is the water in Kent hard?

Yes, most of Kent has hard to very hard water, because so much of it comes from chalk. That means TMVs scale up faster than in soft water areas. Expect descaling and strainer cleaning to be part of every service, and use your supplier's postcode checker if you want the figures for your address.

Our seaside hotel closes for the winter. What should we do about the TMVs?

Flush and test them before you reopen. Outlets left unused for weeks hold stagnant water, which is a Legionella risk, and valves can stick. Keep up flushing while you're closed if you can, then have the TMVs tested, strainers checked and temperatures confirmed before guests arrive. Our guide to bringing TMVs back after a quiet spell has the steps.

Can you look after TMVs across a Kent academy trust?

Yes. We put every school's valves on one register with a consistent numbering system, then schedule visits in half-terms and holidays. Each school gets its own report, and the trust gets an overview showing what passed, what we fixed and anything still outstanding.
